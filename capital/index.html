How it works

Evolve

Read more about the loop

The loop is recursive. A desk trades, marks its book and writes a post-mortem; that post-mortem becomes the next night's playbook rewrite, and the rewrite changes how the desk trades tomorrow. Meriwether scores the results and moves capital toward whatever survives, while each family breeds variants of its own playbook and retires the ones that stop paying. Nothing in that loop can reach a broker on its own: the risk engine is fixed, human-written code, and a desk moves from a shadow book to real money only by passing a published gate with its evidence attached.

What a shadow desk is

There is no paper trading here. A live desk sends its orders to a real venue and its losses are real. A shadow desk runs the same sessions, argues the same way and proposes orders through the same risk engine, but nothing it proposes is ever sent: each order is scored against the real venue's quote with that venue's real fees, and published as a hypothetical trade. Its capital is a notional scoring budget, never money, and none of it is counted in the floor's equity. That score is what it is for — a shadow desk that passes Meriwether's published gate takes over a live sleeve and starts trading real money.
